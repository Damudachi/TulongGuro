01 — INPUT
What the study draws upon
Knowledge Base
Justificatory theory the design answers to:
  • Teacher workload as a structural barrier to timely formative assessment
  • Feedback value residing in timeliness and qualitative substance, not scores alone
  • The Human-in-the-Loop principle — educational AI supports, never replaces, teacher judgment
Policy and Curriculum
DepEd Order No. 8, s. 2015 — component weights (WW / PT / QA), performance descriptor bands, transmutation tables.
DepEd Grade 6 English curriculum standards and competencies.
Empirical Materials
  • Grade 6 English rubrics in use at the participating schools, validated by Head Coordinators
  • Four competency domains derived by grouping those rubric criteria, not imposed externally
  • Teacher-established reference grades
  • Baseline manual grading-time logs
  • Learner constructed responses — handwritten, typewritten, digital
  • Institutional, section and roster data
Technical and Environmental Context
A pre-trained multimodal vision-language model accessed by API — the study's AI work is model selection and prompt engineering, not model training.

The design environment: large classes, paper-based outputs, intermittent connectivity, mixed-device rather than 1:1 classrooms.

02 — PROCESS
Design Science Research cycle · Agile within Design and Development
1
Problem Explication
The grading-workload problem and its cost to feedback quality, established from literature and from practicing teachers.
▼
2
Requirements Definition
Policy, pedagogy and the HITL constraint translated into requirements for the artifact.
▼
3
Design and Development
Agile sprints. Progressive web application; three role-specific interfaces; Human-in-the-Loop review architecture; AI-assisted checking pipeline; iterative prompt and rubric engineering. Pipeline parameters are specified in Methods.
▼
4
Demonstration
Closed Alpha technical benchmarking, then a one-week Beta field deployment across three elementary schools — two DepEd public, one private — with three Grade 6 English teachers.
▼
5
Evaluation
Four measurement strands, one per objective:
Grading reliabilityRaw AI score vs teacher reference grade, both banded: accuracy, per-band precision and recall, macro F1, MAE, normalized MAE, mean signed deviation
Perceived usabilitySystem Usability Scale, administered separately to the users of each interface
Grading workloadQuasi-experimental paired comparison of manual vs assisted time — per paper, per class set, and turnaround
Quality and privacyISO/IEC 25010:2023 weighted evaluation; Data Privacy Officer implementation assessment
Directional claim. Raw AI reliability is a property of the model; workload reduction is the outcome of interest; HITL review is the mediating condition between them. Workload falls without autonomous accuracy because the review gate converts an imperfect draft into a defensible grade.

03 — OUTPUT
What the study produces — evaluated knowledge, not screens
Evaluated Artifact
A tri-interface LMS gradebook: Administrator scaffolding · Teacher gradebook and HITL workspace · Learner dashboard.

A paper is collected PENDING, stays PENDING while the AI drafts against a human-authored rubric, becomes GRADED only on teacher validation, and is seen by the learner only when the reviewed set is released. Every transition is written to an append-only audit log.
Artifact contribution
Empirical Findings
  • Agreement of raw AI grades with teacher reference grades
  • Perceived usability of each interface
  • Grading time and feedback turnaround, manual vs assisted
  • AI processing time per paper
Descriptive, within stated bounds
Design Knowledge
Transferable propositions that outlive this build:
  • At current VLM reliability, the value lies in decision support, not autonomous grading
  • The review gate is the condition of a grade's defensibility, not a precaution added to it
  • Withholding publication until a set is complete protects consistency across a class
  • Viability in low-resource classrooms requires designing for mixed devices and intermittent connectivity from the outset
Prescriptive knowledge
Recommendations
For system development, for schools and DepEd, and for further research.

Feedback — two loops, at different levels
Research-level: DSR iteration
OUTPUT → 02 Requirements Definition & Design
Findings from Demonstration and Evaluation return to requirements and design, so each cycle refines the artifact and its requirements together. This is the loop that makes the study iterative.
Artifact-level: adaptive few-shot prompting
inside 03 Design and Development — self-loop
Channel 1 · teacher-adjusted evaluations. A validated override of ≥5 points, or a substantive feedback edit, stores the AI draft and the teacher's revision as a calibration pair; a moving window of the 3 most recent pairs for that teacher, activity type and grade level is injected into later prompts.
Channel 2 · section context baseline. The 3 most recently approved submissions from the same section supply demonstrations of that cohort's level. A mechanism inside the artifact — not a research activity, and not the arrow that carries findings back into design.
BOUNDARY CONDITIONS. Grade 6 English · rubric-scored constructed responses, not answer-key instruments · decision support, not autonomous grading · dependence on a third-party cloud vision-language model whose pricing, uptime and policies lie outside the researchers' control · three teachers across three schools over one week. These bound the range over which the framework's propositions are asserted to hold.
