Philippine public school teachers face class sizes and paper-based workloads that make timely, personalized feedback nearly impossible, while existing Learning Management Systems act as numerical ledgers and assume a one-to-one device ratio unsuited to low-resource classrooms. This study developed and evaluated TulongGuro, a multi-role LMS gradebook delivered as a progressive web application with Teacher, Student, and Admin interfaces. Server-side image optimization prepares photographed or uploaded work for a cloud Vision-Language Model (VLM) that reads Grade 6 English outputs and evaluates them against the teacher's rubric in a single inference step. A Human-in-the-Loop (HITL) architecture governs every result: teachers retain final authority over each AI draft, and validation and release are separate acts, so a grade reaches a learner only after the teacher publishes the reviewed set. Development followed Design Science Research, with Agile governing the software lifecycle and prompt engineering. Evaluation involved Grade 6 English teachers, Head Coordinators as domain experts, learners, and quality-assurance evaluators across two DepEd public schools and one private school. The system obtained an ISO/IEC 25010:2023 rating of 4.35/5.00 ("Very Good") and System Usability Scale scores of 80, 75, and 84 for the Teacher, Student, and Admin interfaces. Grading time fell from 5.05 to 2.22 minutes per paper and turnaround from 2.55 to 1.50 days, and teachers strongly agreed with the system's pedagogical relevance and practical applicability (M = 4.48). Raw AI drafts matched teacher-established performance bands in only 25.71% of cases, confirming that TulongGuro functions as an AI-assisted decision-support tool rather than an autonomous grader.


Keywords: vision-language models, human-in-the-loop, AI-assisted grading, decision support systems, teacher workload efficiency
